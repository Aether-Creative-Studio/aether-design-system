# Headings

How headings are written across Aether projects: page titles, section headings, card titles, CMS fields and rich text. Applies to new content and to edits of existing content.

## Capitalization

- Sentence case: capitalize the first word and proper nouns only.
- Proper nouns keep their capitals: client and project names, products and tools (Webflow, Figma, Client-First), acronyms (UX/UI, SEO, GDPR), and official program or certification names.
- After a colon, continue in lowercase unless the next word is a proper noun: "Webflow Legacy Editor ending: plan your website update now".

## Trailing periods

- A heading that is a complete sentence, including an instruction, ends with a period: "We start with questions." · "Tell us about your website."
- A label or fragment has no period: "Our studio blog" · "Questions about brand design" · "How we approach web projects".
- Statement-style section headings are written as full sentences rather than fragments: "Structure before visuals." becomes "Structure comes before visuals."
- Keep as labels, without a period: page-name H1s, H1s that carry search terms ("Web design and development in Los Angeles, built to last"), FAQ headings, and "How we approach…" or "What … makes possible" headings.

## Wording

- "and", not "&". Keep "&" only inside proper names.
- Serial comma: "SEO, AEO, and GEO".

## Structure

- One H1 per page. Demote any other H1 to the level its place in the outline calls for.
- Don't repeat the page title as a heading directly under the H1.
- Heading levels follow the outline, not the visual size. Size comes from type styles, never from choosing a different level.

## Aether Creative Studio

Project-specific terms, used verbatim:

- "capability statement", never "capabilities statement"
- Newsletter sign-up heading: "Sign up for our newsletter."
- Certifications: singular name with the acronym in parentheses, such as "Local Small Business Enterprise (LSBE)". "Small Business Enterprise (Proprietary)" and "Small Business Enterprise (Los Angeles)" keep their qualifier and take no acronym.
